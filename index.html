About the Project

This website presents the four assignments completed as part of the Digital Humanities coursework. The work explores how Natural Language Processing (NLP), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), language models, and computational reading practices can support the study and preparation of literary and Indic-language texts.

The projects move from dialect-aware text preprocessing to the extraction of ecological elements from literary works, the synthetic reading of a short story, and the design of reusable instructions for an LLM.

Task 1

NLP – Bankura Bengali Stop Words

Completed

This task develops a curated stop-word list for the Bankura dialect of Bengali (Bankuri / Western Rarh Bengali). The list includes dialect-specific and functional forms such as pronouns, demonstratives, postpositions, conjunctions, auxiliary forms, and discourse particles.

The resource can be used during NLP text preprocessing. The accompanying code also notes that the list should be adapted to the requirements of a particular corpus or research task.

Task 2

Extraction of Indian Cultural Elements Using RAG

Completed

This task uses a Retrieval-Augmented Generation workflow to identify Indian flora and fauna in five Bengali novels available in English translation:

  • The Home and the World – Rabindranath Tagore
  • Srikanta – Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay
  • Gora – Rabindranath Tagore
  • Anandamath – Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay
  • Kapalkundala – Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay

The pipeline prepares the literary corpus, divides the texts into overlapping 180-word segments, and uses TF-IDF vectorization with cosine similarity to retrieve relevant botanical and zoological passages. The retrieved passages are assembled into a grounded prompt for Google Gemini. The final Python deliverable organizes extracted flora and fauna and includes a novel-by-novel provenance index.

Task 3

Synthetic Reading – Rabindranath Tagore’s “Subha”

Completed

This task applies a synthetic-reading approach to Rabindranath Tagore’s short story “Subha” (1918). A Python script divides the story into three parts and selects a reproducible random sample of 20% of the sentences from each part, preserving their original order.

The sampled text is then used for thematic extraction. The analysis groups passages under three themes: phonocentric ableism and social stigma; embodied and multispecies affective language; and patriarchal commodification and caste orthodoxy. The accompanying interpretation discusses these themes through Critical Disability Studies, affective economies, and multispecies/ecofeminist perspectives.

Task 4

Creating a Skill for an LLM – Indic Corpus Preprocessing

Completed

This task develops a reusable LLM skill for preprocessing Indic-language and dialectal literary texts, including Bankura/Rarh Bengali. It provides instructions for removing metadata and boilerplate, applying Unicode normalization, filtering dialect-aware stop words, and preserving culturally significant vocabulary.

The skill also specifies structured output requirements and emphasizes script fidelity and avoiding invented or altered dialect forms.
